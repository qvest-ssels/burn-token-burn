savings & break-even

For: the question people ask once they trust the runway numbers — would a box in the corner be cheaper? Read the caveats before the price.

Running a model on your own hardware is not automatically cheaper. break-even replays your actual usage history against a piece of hardware to find the point where it would have paid for itself; savings compares the cost of local inference — hardware amortisation plus the electricity it actually draws — against cloud API pricing for the same volume of tokens.

Break-even against your real history

$ token-finops break-even --hardware mac-studio-m4-max-128gb --since 90d --replaceable-tiers haiku,sonnet
replaceable cloud usage (haiku/sonnet-class): 35.5M tokens = $14.94 API-equivalent
local alternative: energy $14.29 + capex $116.87 = $131.15
-> cloud still cheaper by $116.21; at the current pace the box never pays off

A Mac Studio at 20 % vs. 80 % utilisation, the same machine on a solar feed-in tariff, and the break-even replay.

Per-token comparison

$ token-finops savings --hardware mac-studio-m4-max-128gb --model qwen3-32b --utilization 0.2
$ token-finops savings --power solar-de-feed-in --utilization 0.8

What to notice: utilisation dominates. The same hardware looks like a bad deal at 20 % and a good one at 80 %, because the purchase price is amortised over the tokens you actually generate. The electricity price matters too, which is why the tariff is a switch rather than a constant.

Already own the box?

Then its purchase price is a sunk cost, and amortising it per token answers a question you no longer have. --own-hardware drops capex entirely and compares the marginal cost of the next token — electricity against the API bill — which is the only number still open to a decision.

$ token-finops savings --own-hardware --power solar-de-feed-in
  capex:             EXCLUDED (--own-hardware): $3,999 is sunk
  local marginal:    $0.08 / 1M tok  -> LOCAL CHEAPER (0.03x)

Green IT: CO2 per 1M tokens

Optional, via --co2. It puts the local emissions figure next to an estimate for the cloud side, and is careful to show that the two are not the same kind of number:

$ token-finops savings --co2 --power solar-de-feed-in --cloud-region us-ercot
  local:  ~    40 g   [computed]  1.00 kWh/1M tok x 40 g/kWh
  cloud:  ~   226 g   [ESTIMATE]  600 Wh/1M tok accelerator-side x PUE 1.13 x 334 g/kWh
  -> local emits ~0.18x the cloud estimate per token (local is cleaner)
  !! The cloud figure is an ORDER-OF-MAGNITUDE ESTIMATE, not a measurement.

--cloud-region picks which grid the cloud tokens are assumed to burn on — us-avg (350 gCO2/kWh, the default), us-virginia (270), us-ercot (334) or de-grid (344), the last so you can compare cloud against local hardware on the same grid and isolate the hardware difference from the grid-mix one.

The same comparison against two different grids: Northern Virginia, then a German solar tariff against Texas.

The local number is arithmetic on your box's wattage and a published grid-mix figure. The cloud number is reconstructed from published per-query energy estimates divided by an assumed response length, because no cloud AI provider publishes energy per token — it is honest to roughly a factor of three, and the output says so rather than hiding it in a footnote. On the default settings (German grid, US average) local emits more per token than the cloud estimate: a hyperscale datacentre batches work across many users and a box on your desk does not. Local wins on carbon mainly when it runs on your own panels. Neither figure counts embodied carbon or water.

Where those numbers come from, and why they are this hard to pin down, is covered on the CO2 research page, which points at the full sourced working in docs/CO2_ESTIMATE.md.

Three honesty caveats

  • Capex is amortised on actual inference — a box sitting idle 80% of the time does not get to spread its purchase price over hours it wasn't working.
  • Solar is priced at the feed-in tariff you forgo, not at zero — running the box on your own panels has an opportunity cost, not a free lunch.
  • Only Haiku/Sonnet-class work counts as replaceable by a local model — a 32B model on your desk does not do Opus/Fable-class work, so break-even only replays the tier of usage a local model could plausibly have done instead.

Measure your own box

Hardware profiles and energy prices live in editable JSON, and every figure carries a source and a review date in docs/sources.md — if a number could not be sourced, it is marked as an estimate rather than quietly presented as fact.

$ token-finops savings --list                              # available hardware/model/tariff/region keys

--co2, --cloud-region and --own-hardware landed after the 0.3.0 release and are not in the published wheel yet — see the install notes. Everything else on this page is in 0.3.0.

Editing your own wattmeter numbers into savings/hardware_profiles.json and savings/energy.json is an explicitly supported workflow, or point the CLI at your own copies without touching the packaged files:

export TOKEN_FINOPS_HARDWARE_JSON=~/my-hardware.json
export TOKEN_FINOPS_ENERGY_JSON=~/my-energy.json

Recordings are asciicast v2 files committed in docs/casts/ — play them locally with asciinema play <file>. This page ships no analytics and no tracking.
